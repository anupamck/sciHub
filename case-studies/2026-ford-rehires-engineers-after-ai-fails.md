## Why I saved this

- A high-profile case of AI automation backfiring when deployed without capturing the institutional knowledge of the humans it replaced. Relevant to understanding the limits of AI in domains where tacit knowledge matters.

## Key points

- Ford hired, promoted, or rehired ~350 veteran technical specialists over three years after AI quality systems underperformed. Many were retired Ford employees or engineers from supplier companies. Internally nicknamed "gray beard" engineers.
- Ford's AI tools (AiTriz for component inspection, MAIVs for manufacturing data analysis, plus computer vision systems) failed because experienced engineers left before their tacit knowledge was transferred into training data. The AI amplified weak inputs rather than catching design flaws.
- VP Charles Poon: "AI is fantastic, but only as good as the information used to train it." He acknowledged Ford "mistakenly believed it could swap in AI and still produce a high-quality product."
- Ford issued 152 recalls in 2025. In 2026, 51 recalls covering 11+ million vehicles — more than double the next manufacturer.
- Ford shed ~5,300 salaried positions since 2020. Detroit automakers collectively cut over 20,000 white-collar jobs in the same period.
- After the rehiring initiative, Ford achieved its highest JD Power Initial Quality ranking in 16 years — #1 mainstream automaker with 152 problems per 100 vehicles (ahead of Nissan and Buick).
- Seven of Ford's top 10 models now rank in the top three of their segments. F-150, Mustang, and Super Duty won best-in-segment for the second consecutive year.
- CEO Jim Farley said the improvements contributed "hundreds and hundreds of millions of dollars" in cost savings via reduced warranty and recall costs.

## What the rehired engineers do

- Run mandatory weekly design reviews as internal auditors.
- Hunt for failure points before blueprints reach the factory floor.
- Mentor younger engineers and transfer institutional knowledge.
- Rebuild data pipelines feeding AI training systems.
- Refine the automated systems they were originally slated to be replaced by.

## Additional measures

- Created a dedicated 40-person software QA team.
- Added 100,000+ AI-powered automated tests to catch edge cases and revalidate late-stage software changes.

## Key people

- **Jim Farley** — CEO, highlighted cost savings.
- **Kumar Galhotra** — COO, announced the rehiring initiative.
- **Charles Poon** — VP of Vehicle Hardware Engineering, explained the technical failures.

## Notes

- The core lesson isn't that AI failed — it's that AI was deployed as a replacement before the knowledge it needed to succeed was captured. Tacit knowledge (expertise built through decades of solving real-world failures) rarely appears in documentation or engineering specs.
- Ford didn't abandon AI. The rehired engineers now improve the AI systems, creating a human-in-the-loop approach rather than full automation.
- This mirrors a broader pattern: organizations that treat AI as a cost-cutting headcount reduction tool rather than an augmentation layer tend to see quality degrade before they course-correct.
